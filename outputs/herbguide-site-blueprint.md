## Herb Library Index Structure

The herb index should be filterable and easy to scan.

Recommended filter groups:

- By category
- By traditional action
- By food use
- By season
- By flavor
- By nature

Recommended sort options:

- Beginner-friendly
- Most popular
- Recently added
- Food therapy herbs

Recommended herb card structure:

- English common name
- Pinyin
- Chinese characters
- short plain-English summary
- tags like `warming`, `tea`, `digestive`, `kitchen-friendly`

## Newsletter Product Direction

Recommended newsletter concept:

`The Weekly Herb Note`

Format:

- one herb
- one simple use
- one TCM term explained
- one caution or sourcing note

Length:

- 3 to 5 minutes to read

Best timing:

- once a week at launch

Good future lead magnets:

- `10 Chinese Herbs Every Beginner Should Know`
- `A Simple Guide to Warming and Cooling Foods`
- `7 Easy TCM Tea and Soup Recipes`

Potential long-term monetization paths after the educational phase:

- newsletter sponsorship
- curated ingredient recommendations
- printable herb guides
- beginner email course
- carefully selected affiliate links

## Design Direction Board

The interface should feel like:

- a calm modern editorial magazine
- a botanical field guide
- a warm archival notebook

Recommended UI motifs:

- soft paper background textures
- herb specimen cards
- thin ink-like dividers
- muted label pills
- classic serif headings with clean sans body text

Do not make it feel like:

- a generic supplement brand
- a dropshipping storefront
- a mystical crystal shop

## Non-Negotiable Trust Elements

These should be present from the first version:

- clear disclaimer
- author or editorial identity
- source citations where health or research claims are summarized
- caution notes on every herb page
- separate `traditional use` from `modern research`
- contact page
